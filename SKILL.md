# Product Page Conversion

Build product-detail pages as a sequence that helps a visitor understand the product, want it, and safely complete the purchase. A product page is not a feature inventory: translate the product into a credible answer to the buyer's real problem.

## Start with the available evidence

Before drafting, extract or request only the information necessary to avoid invented claims:

- Product, target customer, use case, variants, price, offer, and fulfillment markets.
- Materials, dimensions, compatibility, care or installation requirements, and supportable product capabilities.
- Existing images, reviews, shipping terms, return policy, guarantees, ratings, and proof points.

If a claim, policy, delivery time, rating, or review cannot be substantiated, label it as a missing asset or use a clearly marked placeholder. Never fabricate social proof, certifications, discounts, delivery promises, or customer reviews.

## Choose the page's persuasive spine

Define one primary customer problem and the product's most valuable outcome. Then select three to five differentiators that answer “why this product instead of an alternative?”

Write each differentiator as:

`Product feature → customer benefit → practical result`

Example: `Water-resistant fabric → protects essentials from spills → less worry during daily commuting.`

Do not give every feature equal weight. The leading benefit should recur consistently from the hero through the call to action, while supporting points prove it.

## Build the page in this order

Adapt the order only when product evidence or the storefront layout makes a change more useful. Keep every conversion function represented.

1. **Hero / buy box:** Product name; one-line outcome-led value proposition; short supporting explanation; primary image or contextual image; price, offer, variant controls, add-to-cart button, and real trust signals such as ratings, shipping, or payment options.
   - A visitor should understand what it is, whom it helps, and why it matters within three seconds.
2. **Problem to solution:** Name the frustrating situation in the customer's language, then show how the product changes it. Avoid beginning with generic claims such as “premium,” “innovative,” or “easy to use.”
3. **Core benefits:** Present the three to five selected feature-benefit-result points. Use specific, observable language rather than vague superlatives.
4. **Visual proof:** Specify the images needed and the question each must answer. Combine product-overview, detail, in-use lifestyle, size/scale, and before/after images as relevant. A visually attractive image that answers no buyer question is low priority.
5. **How it works and product facts:** Explain use, setup, care, what is included, dimensions, compatibility, and relevant limitations before they become reasons to leave.
6. **Customer proof:** Surface authentic reviews with a relevant customer context and concrete outcome. Prefer reviews that mention the use case, fit, quality, or result over short generic praise.
7. **Friction removal:** State shipping origin, dispatch and delivery expectations, tracking, duties if applicable, return/exchange process and window, warranty/support, and payment methods. Put precise policy language ahead of reassuring marketing language.
8. **FAQ:** Answer the remaining product, order, shipping, and after-sales questions without requiring the visitor to contact support.
9. **Closing CTA:** Restate the primary outcome, offer, and key reassurance immediately beside a clear next action.

## Copy and visual standards

- Lead with the desired outcome, then substantiate it with product facts.
- Make pain points recognizable, not melodramatic. Speak to the customer's moment of use, not an abstract persona.
- Use a scene to let customers picture ownership: home, work, travel, fitness, gifting, pet care, or another evidence-based context.
- Give every image a job: explain size, usage, capacity, materials, compatibility, or results.
- Keep key information scannable: concise benefit-led headings, short supporting copy, and tight lists where they improve comparison.
- Match the storefront's existing language, tone, and compliance requirements. Do not make medical, performance, sustainability, or comparison claims that available evidence cannot support.

## FAQ coverage

Include only relevant questions, prioritizing ones that block purchase:

- **Product:** size, fit, compatibility, use, setup, cleaning, care, materials, and limitations.
- **Order:** payment methods, confirmation, changes, and cancellations.
- **Shipping:** origin, dispatch, delivery range, tracking, destination coverage, and duties/taxes.
- **After-sales:** return window, eligibility, return-shipping responsibility, damaged-item process, warranty, and support path.

## Audit output

When reviewing an existing PDP, report in this compact structure:

1. Conversion diagnosis: the most consequential missing answer or mismatch.
2. Prioritized fixes: high / medium / low, each tied to a buyer question.
3. Ready-to-use replacement copy or content briefs for the highest-priority sections.
4. Missing evidence or assets that must be supplied before stronger claims can be made.

Judge success by whether the page progressively answers: What is it? Does it solve my problem? Why choose this one? Can I picture using it? Can I trust it? What happens if delivery or fit goes wrong? Do not treat a page as complete merely because every template section is present.
